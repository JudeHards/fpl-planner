Method and limitations

Training uses 80 completed Premier League matches from gameweeks 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9. The current-team result uses 20,000 simulations with seed 42; its Monte Carlo standard error is 0.09 points. This measures simulation noise only, not model accuracy.

Transfer search: 1,907 1-transfer, 1,783 2-transfer, 7,780 3-transfer packages evaluated in full. Every single swap is tested. Larger packages are searched by branch and bound: players beaten on price and every week's projection are skipped, and branches whose optimistic bound cannot reach the top results are cut. The bound is not strict for bench cover, so very close alternatives can be missed.

Transfer-screen assumptions

  • Bank is £3.4m and 2 free transfer(s), from bank from current-team.md, free transfers from current-team.md.
  • Markdown prices are provisional selling proceeds unless overridden with --selling-prices JSON. FPL selling prices can differ from market prices because only half of a price rise is retained.
  • Incoming prices are from the latest eligible local playerstats snapshot, already in £m. No price-change forecasts are made.
  • All moves happen before the first gameweek; the resulting squad is held for the entire horizon. Hits cost four points per transfer beyond the supplied free transfers, paid once. No chips or later transfers are modelled.
  • All candidate fitting uses one pre-target training cutoff. Later weeks change fixtures only; availability and minute distributions remain fixed.
  • Deterministic screening integrates the same fitted rates and minute distributions as the simulator, but omits squad-dependent goal/assist share normalisation. Compare every transfer gain with the analytic hold baseline, not the Monte Carlo total.
  • Each week selects the highest projected legal XI and its highest projected captain/vice; bench effects are evaluated after XI selection. Captain fallback and legal autosubs use independent appearance probabilities.
  • Bonus is an independent historical mean, xA approximates FPL assists, and Poisson assumptions approximate defensive contributions, saves and rare events. Current availability may double-count historical injury absence.
  • Every eligible single swap is examined. Packages of two to 3 transfers are searched exhaustively by branch and bound: a player is skipped only when enough others in the same position cost no more and project at least as many points every week, and a branch is cut only when an optimistic bound cannot reach the best 10 of its size. Dominance ignores appearance-driven bench cover, so near-ties may differ slightly from a brute-force search.
  • Candidates need at least one positive-minute history row and a usable price. Unknown new arrivals are omitted; positive gains are estimates rather than guaranteed improvements.
  • Holding saves a transfer for future flexibility, which has no explicit points value in this fixed-horizon model. Very small positive gains should be treated cautiously.
  • Rules reference: https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/2174907 (checked 9 October 2026).

Current-team simulation assumptions

  • No chips or transfer-hit deductions. One target gameweek, including all its fixtures.
  • Automatic XI maximises individual pilot projections within legal formations; bench effects are evaluated afterwards.
  • Minute samples include historical nonappearances; applying current availability can double-count injury absence.
  • Player rates shrink toward position means; team strength shrinks toward league venue means. Current season only.
  • xA approximates FPL assists; unowned teammates absorb unused goal/assist probability.
  • Bonus is drawn independently from historical appearance distributions, not a match-wide BPS ranking.
  • Defensive actions and rare events use independent Poisson rates. Dismissal timing and bench cards are not modelled.
  • Percentiles describe simulated match variation, not uncertainty in estimated rates or future team news.
  • Training excludes multi-fixture player-gameweeks; future doubles are simulated match by match.

The Monte Carlo model shares goal events within a match; the faster screening model uses marginal expectations and independent player appearance probabilities. Neither method has been backtested or calibrated. Future injury recovery, transfers, price changes and unobserved rotation risk are not forecast.
